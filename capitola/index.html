How it works and how it has done

The flood index is still water at the Monterey tide gauge plus wave runup on Capitola Beach (Stockdon 2006), fed by CDIP's nearshore wave model just off the beach, so swell direction and period are built in. Levels come from documented floods: Watch 11 ft (waves reach the seawall), Flood 12.5 ft (water over it onto the Esplanade), Severe 15.5 ft (damaging flooding of Esplanade businesses).

The winter outlook pairs this winter's predicted tides and Kelvin-wave offset with swell and storm surge from the El Niño winters of 2002–03, 2009–10, 2015–16 and 2023–24; run on past winters it closely matches the days that actually crossed each line. The creek model predicts the peak at the Soquel gauge from the day's rain, the day before and the past month's rain at Ben Lomond; it caught the Dec 2022 and Jan 2023 floods as more likely than not and missed March 2016, when far more rain fell in the hills than the gauge saw.

Not modeled: storm-drain flooding (the 2011 Noble Gulch culvert failure), beach sand (a stripped beach lets waves reach the seawall more easily) and new Kelvin-wave pulses. The model reruns every morning; the "Right now" numbers load straight from NOAA, CDIP, USGS and the NWS. Model code and notes.
