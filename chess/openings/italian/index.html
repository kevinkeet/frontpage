Italian Game

C50–C54you play White

After 1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.Bc4, White develops naturally, aims the bishop at Black's weakest square (f7), and castles quickly. The Italian teaches every core opening principle while keeping real attacking chances — it is the single best first opening for improving players.

Lines to study

1.Giuoco Pianissimo (Main Line)

●○○New

The modern main line: White builds slowly with c3 and d3, castles, and keeps all the pieces on for a long strategic fight. This is what top grandmasters actually play.

📖 Learn⚡ Drill

2.vs Two Knights: the solid 4.d3

●○○New

After 3...Nf6 Black invites sharp Fried Liver chaos with 4.Ng5. You sidestep all of it: 4.d3 transposes to the same healthy Pianissimo structure you already know.

3.Evans Gambit (attacking weapon)

●●○New

4.b4!? — sacrifice a pawn to seize the center with tempo. The romantic-era attack that teaches the value of time and initiative. Play this when you want to practice attacking chess.

🔒 Unlocks at Level 3 · Bishop

Why play it

  • Every move follows a principle you can name: center, development, king safety.
  • The bishop on c4 eyes f7 — the one square only Black's king defends — so tactical themes appear early and teach you to calculate.
  • The modern d3 setups (the 'Giuoco Pianissimo') give you a rich middlegame plan you can play for years, all the way to master level.

Key principles

  • Fight for the center with e4 and (eventually) d4 or the slow c3+d3 build.
  • Develop knights before bishops, and develop toward the center.
  • Castle before move 8 in almost every line.
  • Don't grab material before finishing development.

Common mistakes

  • Playing Ng5 too early hunting the f7 fork without backup

    After 3...Nf6 4.Ng5?! d5! Black returns the pawn structure blow — 4.Ng5 is a real weapon but only if you know the theory. As a learner, 4.d3 keeps every advantage of the position without memorization.

  • Blocking the c-pawn with an early Nc3 in c3 lines

    The whole point of the modern Italian is c2-c3 preparing d3-d4 later. Nc3 gives that plan away; the knight belongs on d2 heading to f1-g3.

  • Trading the c4 bishop for a knight without reason

    That bishop is your best attacker. Meet ...Na5 ideas with Bb3 or retreat rather than trading it off cheaply.
